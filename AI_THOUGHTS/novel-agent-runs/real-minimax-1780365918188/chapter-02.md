# Chapter 2

The rain had stopped by dusk, leaving the cobblestones slick and the air thick with petrichor. Elena stood at the window of the old maritime office, watching lanterns flicker to life along the harbor. Three weeks since the letter arrived. Three weeks of sleepless nights and half-packed trunks.

She turned from the glass. The room smelled of salt and mildew, of documents no one had read in decades. On the desk, her father's seal pressed into red wax, the only proof that any of this was real.

Downstairs, the floorboards groaned. She froze, hand drifting toward the desk drawer where she kept her father's service revolver. The footsteps paused. Then continued, unhurried, ascending.

Elena did not move. The door handle turned slowly, and through the gap she saw the shadow of a wide-brimmed hat, the curve of a walking stick.

She had not sent for anyone.

The door swung open. A woman stood in the frame, rain-damp and smiling, wearing the faded insignia of a regiment that had been dissolved twenty years before Elena was born.

"You received my message," the stranger said. It was not a question.
