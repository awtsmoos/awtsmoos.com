# Chapter 16

The lighthouse had stood abandoned for thirty years, its white paint peeling like old skin, its beam extinguished long before anyone could remember. Miriam climbed the spiral stairs anyway, her knees protesting, her breath catching at each turn. At the top, she found what the estate agent had not mentioned: the keeper's log, still open on the desk, its final entry dated September 3, 1987.

She did not read it. Instead, she stood at the salt-crusted window and watched the fog roll across the channel, swallowing the village below, then the hills, then the sky itself. Somewhere beneath that grey shroud, her mother's cottage still stood, empty now, waiting for whatever the lawyers decided. It no longer mattered.

Miriam understood finally what the lighthouse had been trying to tell her all those summers, watching her build sandcastles at its base: some things are meant to warn rather than guide. She placed her hand against the cold glass and let it rest there, a small warmth against the ancient stone.

Below, the sea continued its patient work, shaping the shore for someone else's children.
