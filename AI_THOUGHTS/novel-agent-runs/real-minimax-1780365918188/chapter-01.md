# Chapter 1

The Cartographer's Last Voyage

The ink had dried on three continents before Elias Vane stopped sleeping.

His studio on Rue des Cartographes smelled of copper and sea salt, a permanent fog that clung to the vellum charts pinned across every wall. At fifty-three, his hands still traced the coastline of memory with the precision of a surgeon, though his eyes had begun to betray him—seeing harbors that no longer existed, trade winds that shifted centuries ago.

Tonight, the candles guttered. Outside, the Seine whispered against its banks like a dying man's final confession. Elias unrolled the final commission: a blank sea, untouched parchment, waiting for him to imagine what no explorer had ever found.

The admiral wanted routes to the Unknown Shore—a place that existed only in fever dreams and dying sailors' last words.

Elias dipped his quill. His hand trembled.

Somewhere in the darkness of that unmarked ocean, his drowned daughter was still sailing home.

He began to draw the way back.
