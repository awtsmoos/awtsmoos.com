# Chapter 5

The rain had not stopped for three days.

Elena stood at the window of the lighthouse keeper's cottage, watching the Atlantic churn beneath a sky the color of wet slate. The storm had driven the fishing boats to harbor, and with them, the only source of news from the mainland. She pressed her palm against the cold glass, tracing the path of a droplet as it slid toward the sill.

Her father had been dead for seven months. The letters had stopped arriving six weeks ago.

The keeper's wife, Marta, set down a cup of tea that sent steam curling toward the ceiling. "The morning ferry might make it through," she said, though her voice carried no conviction.

Elena turned from the window. On the table lay her father's last letter, its edges soft from handling. Three words in his careful script: *Come home now.*

She did not know what home meant anymore.

But she gathered her coat, her small bag, and stepped into the gray morning where the wind tried to steal her breath. The pier waited, slick with rain. And beyond it, the mainland held answers she was not certain she wanted.
