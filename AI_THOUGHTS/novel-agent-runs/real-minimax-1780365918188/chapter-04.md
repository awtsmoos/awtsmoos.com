# Chapter 4

The river ran black beneath the bridge where Elena found him. Jonas sat on the concrete ledge, boots dangling over the current, cigarette ember glowing faint as a dying star. She recognized the posture—the way his shoulders curved inward like wings folded against some perpetual wind.

"You're going to fall in," she said.

"Mm." He didn't turn. "Maybe I want to."

Elena settled beside him, close enough that their elbows nearly touched. The city hummed behind them, indifferent and enormous. Somewhere a siren wailed, then faded into nothing.

Her sister's name hung between them, unspoken. Three weeks since the funeral, and they still couldn't say it. The word had become a locked door neither would touch.

"She left a letter," Jonas said quietly. "I found it yesterday. Inside her coat pocket." He reached into his jacket and withdrew a folded square of paper, edges soft with age. "She wrote it months before."

The water whispered below. Elena's hands trembled at her sides.

"I haven't read it," he added. "Seemed wrong, without you."
