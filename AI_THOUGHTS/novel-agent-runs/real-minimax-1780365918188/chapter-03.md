# Chapter 3

The letter arrived three days after the funeral, its envelope yellowed and water-stained. Maren recognized her grandmother's handwriting—loops like birds in flight—and her hands trembled as she broke the seal.

Inside, a single photograph fell loose. A cliff. A lighthouse. A date marked in violet ink.

*You were never meant to find this*, the letter began. *But the truth follows bloodlines like rivers find the sea.*

Maren read it twice, then a third time. The words spoke of a house built into the崖 cliff's edge, of doors that opened onto nothing, of her grandmother's sister who vanished one winter without a trace. The letter ended mid-sentence, as if torn away.

Rain struck the window behind her. Outside, the lighthouse on Blackmoor Point flickered twice—a pattern she now understood as language.

She folded the letter, placed it against her heart, and began to pack.

Some inheritances are written in blood. Others in longing. And some, like this one, demand the living return to the places the dead could not leave.
