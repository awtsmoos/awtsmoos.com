# Chapter 10

The river had swallowed three villages by dawn.

Elena stood on the wreckage of a footbridge, watching the current tear through her hometown like something hungry. Water lapped against second-floor windows. Somewhere beneath the surface, her mother's voice waited—a memory now, not a person. The flood had come without warning, or perhaps with warnings no one had believed.

She waded through knee-deep muck toward the old lighthouse at the village edge. Father Marden had retreated there three days ago, convinced the water was divine punishment. Elena needed his maps. Needed to understand why the river had changed course so violently, so precisely, as if something had forced it.

Her boots slipped on submerged steps. Cold water seeped through fabric, pressed against skin. Above her, the lighthouse beam still turned—automatic, indifferent to catastrophe.

A rope ladder clung to the tower's exterior. She grabbed it, felt rust flaking beneath her grip, and began to climb toward a man who claimed to know what the river wanted.

The water kept rising below. The beam kept turning. And somewhere in the distance, she heard something that might have been singing.
