# Chapter 14

The rain had stopped three hours ago, but the village still wept. Water dripped from the eaves of the church, pooling in the cracked stones of the courtyard where they gathered.

Margaux recognized the shape beneath the tarp before anyone spoke. Thomas. They had brought him home.

She stood apart from the others, her hands raw from the rope that had snapped her from the mast two nights before. The sea had given him back darker than she remembered. Darker, and incomplete.

"He came to us first," the harbor master said. His voice carried the careful weight of official report. "Told us about the storm, the wreck. Said you were the only survivor."

Margaux felt the eyes turn toward her. The village had always whispered about her voyages with Thomas—the woman who refused to stay, the man who refused to let her go.

"He lied," she said.

The words fell like stones into water. Somewhere behind her, a child began to cry.
