# Chapter 11

The rain had not stopped in three days.

Maren stood at the window of the old lighthouse, watching the sea devour itself. Below, the village slept behind wet glass—dim shapes of shuttered homes, a church spire dissolved into mist. Her hands smelled of salt and copper. She had gutted the last of the catch an hour ago, and her fingers still trembled from the cold.

She did not belong here. She knew this the way one knows a scar—intimately, without looking.

The keeper had left six months back, fled in the night with debts heavier than anchors. He'd told her, before he vanished: the light still works. Keep it lit. Someone will come.

No one had come.

Maren pulled her coat tighter. Through the spiral stairs behind her, she heard the mechanism of the clockwork turn—ticking, eternal, indifferent. She climbed down into the lamp room and struck the flame.

The glass caught it. For a moment, the whole sea turned gold.
