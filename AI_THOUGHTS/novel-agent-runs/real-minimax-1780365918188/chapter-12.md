# Chapter 12

The rain had not stopped for three days. Mara pressed her back against the cold stone archway, watching the river swell beyond the bridge's edge. The village below had become a memory of itself—silent doors, flooded cellars, the faint smell of wet ash from hearths they no longer lit.

She had been waiting since dawn. The boy had promised to return by noon with news from the capital, but the roads had turned to mud and the crossing to nothing but a guessing game.

Her fingers found the worn edge of the letter in her pocket again. Twenty-three words. She had read them so many times the syllables had lost meaning, become only sound. She needed them to mean something again.

A figure emerged from the tree line, moving slow, deliberate—too slow. Not running. Not her brother.

Mara stepped forward into the rain, hands empty at her sides, as the stranger's face slowly resolved into recognition and the weight of what he carried in his silence.
