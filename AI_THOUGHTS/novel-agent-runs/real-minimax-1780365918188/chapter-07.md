# Chapter 7

The morning Elena found the letter tucked beneath the floorboard, frost still clung to the windowpanes. She read it twice, her breath visible in the cold room, before folding it into her coat pocket. Three years her father had been dead, yet his handwriting—that cramped, careful script she remembered from childhood permission slips and grocery lists—appeared now like a ghost refusing burial.

She descended the stairs to find Marcus already at the kitchen table, cradling a mug that steamed in the gray light. He looked up when she entered, and something in her expression made him set down the cup.

"He knew," she said. "About the bridge. About what would happen to the town."

Marcus was silent. Outside, the river had begun its annual swell, water darkening the lowlands like a spreading bruise.

"If we dam it," Elena continued, "the flooding reaches the cemetery first. His grave. The whole eastern section."

She watched Marcus absorb this, watched the calculation behind his eyes—the dam, the insurance money, his mother's medical bills, the mathematics of survival that had become their only language.

"We could move him," he offered quietly.

Elena shook her head. "Moving him would wake everything else up."

The clock on the wall ticked. Somewhere in the walls, old pipes groaned with the weight of history.
