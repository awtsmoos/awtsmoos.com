# Chapter 15

The rain had stopped by the time she reached the bridge, but the stones still gleamed wet and treacherous under her boots. Elena gripped the railing and looked down into the river's churning gray. Her reflection shattered and reformed with each current. She had carried his letter for three days, the ink bleeding where her fingers had held it too long.

The lighthouse at the harbor's edge flashed its warning, once, twice. She counted the seconds between each pulse. Fourteen. The same interval her father had taught her as a child, when the fog rolled in and the ships needed to find their way home.

She heard his footsteps before she saw him—measured, unhurried, exactly as he approached everything in life. He stopped three feet behind her. Neither spoke. The wind carried the salt smell of deeper water.

"You received it," he finally said. Not a question.

Elena turned. His coat was dark and shapeless, but she could see the tremor in his hands as he folded them behind his back. "I received it."

Below them, the river moved with the patience of something that had carried many secrets to the sea.
