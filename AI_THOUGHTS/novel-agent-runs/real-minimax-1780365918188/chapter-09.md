# Chapter 9

The rain had stopped by the time Elena reached the lighthouse, but the stones still wept, dark rivulets carving paths through decades of salt and silence. She climbed the spiral stairs with her grandmother's key warm in her palm, each step a memory pressed into the iron.

At the top, the lamp had long since gone dark, but through the curved glass, she could see the whole curve of the bay—the village below, the cemetery where her mother lay, and beyond, the grey nothing where the sea swallowed everything it touched.

Her grandmother had stood here every evening for forty years, watching for a ship that never returned. Her father. The captain who sailed toward a storm he knew would kill him.

Elena understood now. Some people didn't wait for the lost to come home. They simply learned to live in the lighthouse, keeping the light burning in their chests, year after year, until the darkness itself decided to leave.

She sat on the cold floor and waited for dawn.
