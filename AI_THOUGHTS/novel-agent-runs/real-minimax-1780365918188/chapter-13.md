# Chapter 13

The rain had not stopped for three days. It fell in silver sheets against the window of the safehouse, a dull percussion that matched the rhythm of Elena's thoughts. She stood motionless before the cracked mirror, studying the woman who stared back—hollow cheeks, bruised knuckles wrapped in gauze, eyes that held nothing but calculation.

The message had arrived at midnight. Three words: *She's alive. Come.*

Seven years she had believed Clara dead. Seven years of silence, of cemetery flowers sent every anniversary, of learning to breathe around the absence. Now this.

She lifted the packed bag from the bed. Three bullets remained in the revolver. Enough to get her there. Not enough to get her out.

The train station would be empty at this hour. She knew the route—every shadow, every camera, every blind spot between here and the platform. She had memorized it years ago, for exactly this possibility.

The door creaked behind her.

"You're not going alone," said Marcus, his voice rough from sleep. He held a second bag over his shoulder, and his eyes—grey as winter iron—showed no room for argument.

She could argue. She could tell him it wasn't his fight, that the people waiting had no reason to spare him. But something in his stance—a weight she recognized from her own shoulders—stopped the words before they formed.

They descended the stairs together, passing rooms where other survivors slept in shifts, dreaming of worlds they might never see again. Outside, the city exhaled steam and shadows. Somewhere across the river, Clara was waiting.

Elena pulled her collar tight and stepped into the rain.
