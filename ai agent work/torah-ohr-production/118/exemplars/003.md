# The Builders Who Owned No Stone

Tal-Or’s miners called themselves builders, though they erected few houses. Their tunnels shaped where air, water, and people could move. Haggai told them the settlement’s true construction occurred whenever their deeds made room for justice.

Nadav preferred visible monuments. He proposed a stone granary bearing the council names. The seed-bread could be dried, pressed, and stored there despite the overnight spoilage. He argued that builders must leave permanent works.

Uri tested the bread in three ovens. Heat changed its texture but not its law. Portions kept beyond the day bred bitterness by morning. The baker reported honestly. Nadav suppressed the finding and hired Seth to begin foundation records.

Meanwhile, Baruch organized miners to repair a widow’s roof. They used waste boards from ore crates and no commemorative plaque. Rain arrived that night; the patched roof held. Haggai called it a wall in the world’s moral house.

Nadav laughed. “A roof will rot.”

“So will your name on stone.”

The granary foundation disturbed an old drainage trench. Water entered the western sleeping quarter and ruined twenty blankets. Nadav blamed the surveyor. Seth knew Nadav had moved the marked boundary to enlarge the building. He kept silent because the ration master promised him a clerkship.

Families spent the night in the ore hall. The widow whose roof had been repaired brought dry blankets from her house. Her patched, temporary shelter became a source of rescue, while Nadav’s monument made people homeless.

At the morning assembly, Haggai asked who built the world that night: the man laying named stone or the men repairing an anonymous roof. Seth looked at the wet quarter and began to understand the weight of his silence.

Nadav canceled the granary but ordered the foundation left as evidence of future planning. Uri insisted the stones be removed to restore drainage. The whole settlement worked two days undoing the monument.

No one owned the final trench. Water flowed through it because many hands accepted a shared form. The builders possessed no stone, yet their conduct sustained the place. Nadav watched his carved foundation blocks hauled into retaining walls with his name turned inward where no one could see.
