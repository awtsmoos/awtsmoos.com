# A Thousand Steps in Both Directions

The miners had always imagined ascent more easily than descent. A man climbed from the shaft toward daylight; a bucket rose toward the hoist; a student advanced from one page to the next. Haggai’s next lesson disturbed that simple picture.

“*La’alafim* means thousands of gradations,” he told them. “Degree after degree, elevation after elevation.”

Seth thought immediately of the two Rivers Dinur. Haggai continued before he could speak.

“The same multiplicity exists in the drawing downward. From above to below there are contractions after contractions until even the delight of the upper Gan Eden can become a delight of created beings.”

Nadav raised an eyebrow. “Why would giving require so many reductions?”

Haggai pointed toward the smelting house, where white heat from the furnace was divided through flues before it reached the drying rooms. “Do not confuse this mechanism with the worlds. But it can help you understand why unmediated intensity is not the same as communicated benefit.”

A week earlier, Nadav had ordered steam from the main boiler routed directly into a bathhouse line. The pressure burst two pipes. No one was badly hurt, but the walls still bore scald marks.

“Power that cannot be received has not yet become the recipient’s delight,” Uri said.

Haggai returned to the text. The word *alafim*, it taught, was rooted in the *alfin*—the alefs—in the filling of the Divine Name Mah, the Name Havayah filled with alefs. He did not expand the claim beyond the passage. He simply wrote the Hebrew letters and let the men see that “thousands” and “alefs” were being joined as part of the teaching’s structure.

Seth drew two columns in his notebook. Under ASCENT he wrote: level after level, forgetting after forgetting. Under DESCENT he wrote: contraction after contraction, reception after reception.

“Are these two ladders?” he asked.

“Not independent ladders,” Haggai said. “The passage is showing the ordered multiplicity in both directions. The same word *la’alafim* opens the thought.”

That afternoon the settlement tested a new heat system. Baruch installed several pressure chambers between the furnace and the baths. Each chamber reduced what the next could not yet bear. By the time warmth reached the last room, children could put their hands against the pipe without pain.

Nadav stood beside the furnace gauge. “The final warmth is weaker.”

“Measured for the vessel,” Baruch corrected.

Haggai, hearing them, added only what the source allowed: “In the spiritual descent described here, contraction makes possible a delight belonging to created beings. Do not conclude that the Source itself has become small.”

At night Seth walked the forty-nine-lamp stair from the previous festival. He now imagined no final platform from which the world could be surveyed in one simple line. Between any two conditions there might be another measured transition; above, another ascent; below, another fitting of abundance to a receiver.

The word “thousands” no longer sounded to him like a large number. It sounded like patience built into revelation.
