# The Letter Drawn Across the Shaft

Haggai drew a single alef on the mine map.

He placed one yud above, one yud below, and a vav between them. The workers leaned closer. Nadav complained that the mark covered three tunnels.

“That is why I drew it large,” Haggai said.

The passage connected this alef with the alef of the Divine Name *Ehyeh*—“I am destined and ready to be revealed.” Alef, Haggai explained from the text, can serve the future. More than that, the descent of all spiritual flows was described as taking the form of alef.

He touched the upper yud. “First, contraction.”

Then the vav. “Then extension—the flow.”

Then the lower yud. “And we will come to this next.”

Seth asked why revelation should begin with constriction. Would not generosity mean giving without limit?

Haggai answered with the exact logic of the passage: the vav-like extension comes through the contraction represented by the upper yud. Without contraction, there could not be a differentiated drawing and revelation in another manner; everything would remain one undivided matter.

Uri remembered the burst steam line. “So a boundary is not necessarily a refusal.”

“Correct,” Haggai said, “but keep the point precise. Here contraction is what permits a distinct extension and disclosure.”

Nadav was quiet. He had spent years using limits as defenses—locked rooms, secret ledgers, restricted maps. Now the same word, contraction, appeared in a teaching where limitation served revelation rather than concealment.

That day Tal-Or’s engineers faced a practical problem. A high reservoir fed three neighborhoods, but the main channel was too broad to create enough directed pressure for the upper houses. Baruch proposed narrowing the outlet before dividing the stream.

Nadav laughed. “Less opening to give more water?”

“Less opening to make a usable flow.”

They fitted a stone throat to the reservoir. The broad, almost motionless mass of water gathered into a forceful channel and traveled through the distribution pipes. The narrowing did not create the water. It gave the water a form in which it could be directed.

Haggai refused to let the engineers turn their success into proof of heaven. “The reservoir is a mashal at most. The Torah Ohr gives the alef itself as the structure: yud above, vav in the middle, yud below.”

Seth recopied the letter on a clean page. The upper point looked almost like nothing beside the long middle stroke. Yet without that first point, the teaching said, the middle could not become a distinct revelation.

He wrote beneath it: *Future does not mean absent. It can mean prepared for disclosure.*

Haggai read the line and allowed it to remain because it stayed close to the association of *Ehyeh* in the passage.

By evening the upper houses had water. Children filled jars from a stream made possible by a narrowing no one would praise in a song.

On the mine map, the alef still crossed three tunnels. Nadav no longer asked Haggai to erase it.
