# The Wheel That Was Highest and Lowest

The hoist house at Tal-Or had two iron wheels mounted on the same timber frame. One was small enough for Seth to span with both arms. The other rose above his head, circled the smaller wheel, and disappeared through a trench beneath the floor before rising again on the opposite side.

Haggai brought the workers there after morning study. He did not begin with a verse. He put his hand on the outer rim.

“Stand here,” he told Seth.

At the top of the frame, the great wheel stood higher than the small one. Then Haggai led him down the inspection steps into the trench. There, at the bottom of the smaller wheel, the rim of the great wheel passed beneath it.

Nadav frowned. “It is still the greater wheel.”

“Exactly,” Haggai said. “Its greatness is not proved by remaining above.”

He opened the scroll and read the words about the appearance and action of the heavenly wheels: *as a wheel within a wheel*. The image, he explained, was not merely of circles arranged beside one another. The larger encompassing wheel is above the smaller at its height and beneath it at its depth. From a sufficiently encompassing level, what the smaller order calls above and below do not remain absolute opposites.

That was why the passage spoke of *ruach hachayah*—the spirit of the living creature—in the wheels, not “the spirits of the living creatures.” The source being described was singular and exceedingly high. Precisely because it came from such a height, it could descend and spread below. A source trapped in one relative height would still belong to the scale it claimed to transcend.

Nadav glanced toward the mine shaft. “Then the higher thing proves itself by reaching the lower?”

Haggai shook his head. “Do not turn the teaching into a slogan. The text says that before this source, above and below are equal; therefore what is higher can extend downward. The descent does not make the source low.”

That afternoon a storm filled the eastern drainage channel. Water entered the lower galleries faster than hand pumps could lift it. Caleb wanted the large hoist wheel locked in its usual direction to protect the ore lift. Baruch crawled under the frame and found that the same wheel could drive the emergency pump if its lower gearing were engaged.

Nadav objected. The great wheel, he said, had been built to raise loads upward, not to labor in the drainage trench.

Seth looked at the iron rim passing below his boots and remembered the lesson.

They disengaged the ore cages and coupled the great wheel to the lower pump. For three hours the wheel that towered above the house turned through mud and black water beneath the smaller gears. The eastern gallery drained before the supporting beams softened.

No one called the wheel diminished when it came below. Its very circumference allowed it to serve both heights.

At dusk Haggai returned with Seth to the trench. Water still dripped from the stones. Above them, the small wheel was motionless; below it, the outer rim continued its slow final turn.

“Do not confuse the machine with the mystery,” Haggai said. “But remember why the image was chosen. A circle can surround a distinction that a straight ladder cannot escape.”

Seth entered the day in the mine ledger: *The greater wheel passed beneath the lesser and remained greater.* He left the next line blank, unwilling to reduce the teaching further.
