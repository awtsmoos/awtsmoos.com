# When the Wings Fell Still

Before sunrise, Baruch entered the collapsed west gallery with three miners and a lamp. The roof had shifted during the night, and no one knew whether a second fall would follow. They stopped before the broken brace. Baruch closed his eyes and prayed.

Nadav, standing behind him, whispered that fear was wasting time.

Haggai, who had come to inspect the men before they descended, said nothing until Baruch opened his eyes. Then he told them a teaching of the Maggid on the verse, “When they stood, their wings relaxed.” The angels possess love and awe of their own, Haggai explained, yet the love and awe drawn from below in human service can cause their wings to be lowered from their former level before that drawing.

Nadav frowned. “How can the fear of a miner in dust stand in such a comparison?”

Haggai did not yet answer the mechanism. He kept to the movement named by the teaching: human love and awe rising from below, the angelic wings relaxing before that descent of influence, and acts of kindness joining the same pattern of ascent and return.

Inside the gallery, Baruch’s prayer did not remove the danger. He rose and ordered two men back while he tested the brace himself. The timber groaned. He withdrew, changed the angle, and sent for heavier supports. His fear had not paralyzed him; it had become carefulness. When the roof settled, no one was beneath it.

Later that day he carried a food parcel to the family of a miner whose arm had been broken in an earlier fall. Haggai called the deed another movement in Avraham’s path: going and journeying, running upward and returning downward, love and awe entering acts of kindness.

Nadav spent the afternoon calculating how many work hours the delay had cost. The numbers were correct. They simply did not contain what had happened in the gallery.

At evening prayer, the miners stood shoulder to shoulder. Some prayed from gratitude, some from shame, some from fear that the roof would move again. Their bodies ached, their thoughts wandered, and their voices were uneven.

Haggai did not romanticize the imperfection. He returned only to the source’s contrast: the winged service of the angels above and the love and awe drawn through human service below, before which those wings are said to relax.

Nadav watched Baruch’s soot-black hands fold over his heart. For once he had nothing to add to the ledger. The wings of calculation fell still before a movement it could record only after it had already begun.
