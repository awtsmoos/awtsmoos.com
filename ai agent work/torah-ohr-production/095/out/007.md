# Fire Within and Around

Uri fired a new bread oven at the edge of the courtyard. To test the clay, he packed coals inside the chamber and stacked a ring of brushwood around the outer wall. Heat entered from both directions. The vessel glowed without losing its shape.

Haggai brought his students there after evening study.

“Torah learned in bittul,” he said, “draws inwardness. Mitzvot draw surrounding lights and garments.”

Nadav looked at the oven. “Inside and outside?”

Haggai nodded. The source pairs these movements with Mah and Ban and reads the verse “Havayah your God is a consuming fire” as an image of fire within the vessel and fire surrounding it. The purpose was not theatrical flame. It was to distinguish what enters inwardly from what enclothes and encompasses.

During the lesson Seth kept glancing at his sleeves. He had been copying charter passages for weeks and had begun to enjoy being known as Haggai’s clerk. The words were entering his memory, but his conduct had not yet caught up with them. Earlier that day he had refused to carry flour for an injured worker because the task was “beneath a scribe.”

Uri handed him a pair of heavy oven gloves.

“Garments matter,” the baker said.

Seth flushed. He carried the flour.

Haggai used no further rebuke. Torah, he explained, is not only an idea hovering above the learner. Learned with bittul, it is drawn inward. A mitzvah, by contrast, can surround the person as a garment does—an enacted form that holds the body and its powers inside a commanded shape.

Nadav asked whether one was therefore higher than the other.

“The unit does not make that comparison,” Haggai said. “It gives us two modes of drawing: inwardness, and encompassing garments.”

The fire settled. Heat from the outer brush met heat from the inner coals until the clay hardened between them. Uri withdrew the first loaf with blackened gloves.

Seth took it to the injured worker before tasting any himself.

The lesson remained simple enough to stand in the courtyard: an inner fire, an encompassing fire, a vessel held between them. Torah with bittul enters; mitzvot clothe. What the mind receives and what the body performs become two directions of one consuming holiness.
