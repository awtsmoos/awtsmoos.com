# The Words Spoken From Both Sides

Seth thought the teaching would end with fear, kingship, or the shofar. Instead Haggai opened the final source with blessing: “And they bless You; may Your name be blessed by the mouth of every living thing.”

“The wording changes,” Haggai said. “Israel blesses ‘You’—*אותך*—while the continuation speaks of ‘Your name.’ The source makes that difference central.”

Through Torah, given in the five voices already traced, Israel draws not merely a revelation called the Divine Name but the Infinite Himself. This is possible although Torah is learned in physical mouths and Israel lives in a physical world, because before Him darkness and light are equal; physical lowliness does not block the One who is beyond the whole order of high and low.

Nadav asked, “Then the learner reaches upward until he finds Him?”

“The source describes something more wondrous. When a Jew learns Torah, the Holy One learns opposite him. ‘My words that I have placed in your mouth.’ The words are His words placed within the learner’s physical speech.”

Seth read a line aloud. His voice was ordinary: breath, throat, tongue. Haggai did not claim that Seth heard a heavenly echo. The teaching was subtler and greater: Torah study below is the place where the Divine word is drawn into the learner’s mouth.

From that drawing, the blessing continues outward—‘may Your name be blessed by the mouth of every living thing.’ Other created beings receive and bless the Name according to their level. Israel’s Torah draws *You Yourself*, and through that, blessing reaches the Name for all life.

Caleb took the shofar down from the gate one last time. No one blew it. Its narrow mouth, broad opening, voices, fire, water, smoke, fear, and peace had all led here: not away from the physical world, but into words spoken by a living mouth.

Seth bent over the final line of his copy. The settlement was quiet enough to hear his pen. Then he read the words again, and Haggai listened—not for a second voice in the room, but for the mystery the source had taught them to recognize in the first one.
