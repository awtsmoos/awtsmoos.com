# The Note That Carried Delight

The morning after Caleb’s first blast, the people asked why a sound that frightened them belonged beside the teaching of Torah. Haggai did not answer in the study hall. He led them to Uri’s bakery, where a collapsed oven had left the settlement without bread.

All day they repaired it. Baruch reset the stones. Seth copied a fair ration list. Even Nadav, watched closely after his earlier hoarding, carried clay without complaint. At sunset Uri lit the oven again. Warm bread-smell filled the lane, and relief moved through the workers like light.

Haggai took the horn from the gate. “Yesterday you heard the simple sound as fear. Today hear another matter taught by the same shofar.”

He spoke of the call to improve one’s deeds—*shapru ma'aseichem*—and of the teaching that the shofar draws delight from the source of delight. Before anything pleasant can be grasped and enjoyed, its root is higher than the pleasure already felt in a thing. “With You is the source of life,” he said; the source itself is held null beside the Infinite.

Nadav looked at the repaired oven. “So the horn is not merely an alarm?”

“No. Fear can break sleep. But the teaching goes farther. The call to repair what is crooked opens toward a delight whose source is above the delight we already know.”

Caleb sounded one plain note. No tune was added. The children still startled. Yet now the workers stood beside an oven restored by better deeds than those of the day before.

Uri divided the first loaves evenly. Nadav accepted only his portion.

The horn’s voice vanished quickly into the dusk. The bread remained warm in their hands. Haggai let the two facts stand together: a simple sound that awakened fear, and from that narrow awakening, a path toward delight.
