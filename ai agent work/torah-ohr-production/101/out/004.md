# The Heart That Could Not Find an Edge

Seth spent three days measuring the eastern tract of Tal-Or. He returned with cords, pegs, and a neat tablet of distances. Haggai studied it, then asked, “And where does the sky end?”

Seth laughed. “That is not a surveyor’s question.”

“It is today.”

Haggai gathered the apprentices beside the boundary stones. He taught them the phrase, “If your heart runs,” and stressed the word *heart*. Understanding, he said, belongs to the heart when a person truly considers the greatness of the Infinite.

Nadav objected. “We can think about large things. The mountain is larger than the mine. The sky is larger than the mountain.”

“Large still has measure,” Haggai answered. “The teaching is not asking you to imagine a larger boundary. It asks you to contemplate that the Infinite has no end and no limit at all.”

He pointed to Seth’s cord. “A cord can fail because the field is too long. Thought fails differently here. There is no farther peg to reach.”

The workers were quiet. They had built their settlement by measuring: grain by weight, tunnels by cubit, wages by day. Every safety rule depended on edges that could be marked. Haggai did not despise those measures. He asked only that they not mistake measured creation for its Source.

“The verse says His glory is upon earth and heaven,” he continued. “Glory—radiance—not a boundary around Him.”

Baruch wound the survey cord back onto its spool. Nadav, for once, did not try to turn the lesson into an account.

At sunset Seth returned the boundary tablet to its shelf. The numbers remained correct. But beside them he left one blank line. It was not a missing measurement. It was the place where measurement itself had reached its end, and the heart had begun to run.
