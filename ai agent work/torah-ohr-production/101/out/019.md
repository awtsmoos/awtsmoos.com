# The Leech That Emptied Itself

A healer passing through Tal-Or carried a jar of leeches. Nadav recoiled when one fastened to a swollen arm and darkened as it filled. Later, when the creature dropped away exhausted, Haggai remembered the source’s image.

“Egypt’s evil drew and drew,” he said, “until the force by which it oppressed was spent through its own action. The teaching compares this to a leech that sucks blood and in the end dies from what it has taken.”

The point was not that oppression became medicine. Egypt remained Egypt. But Israel’s refinement in the iron furnace prepared more than the people alone. “The world, too, is placed in the human heart,” Haggai said. When Israel was refined, the physical realm through which Israel would serve was opened to a new refinement as well.

That is why the source returns to physical mitzvot after the Exodus: offerings that could be eaten in holiness, the flour of a meal-offering, material acts able to draw the Infinite below. Before Sinai the patriarchs offered ascent-offerings, yet the later pattern of eating sacred offerings and using the full range of physical mitzvah-material had not been given in the same way. Their age also did not yet stand under the later physical Torah-law in identical form; the source points to Jacob’s marriage to two sisters and Amram’s marriage to his aunt as signs of that difference.

Nadav asked whether the furnace ended forever at Sinai.

“No. The teaching compares Egypt, the preparation for Torah, with the later exile under Edom, the preparation for the revelation of Messiah. The mitzvot do not disappear then. Their inward light is revealed more fully, until ‘the earth will be filled with knowledge of God.’”

Uri closed the healer’s jar. Haggai added one final, difficult line: even a person’s private suffering can serve as refinement, clearing obstruction from the way he receives and performs a mitzvah.

No one called pain good. They called refinement costly. The leech had fallen away; the arm remained. And the command that descended after the furnace was meant to enter the very world the furnace had prepared.
