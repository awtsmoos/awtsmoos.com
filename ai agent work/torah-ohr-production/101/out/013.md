# The Last Man Ate What Came First

A failed grain wagon left Tal-Or with two days of bread. Nadav immediately proposed cutting the miners’ portions, arguing that people were the highest creatures and therefore should need less help from what was below them.

Haggai stopped the council before the ration knives came out. “The teaching asks you to notice something strange. Adam was created after the animals and plants, yet human life is sustained by eating them.”

Uri set a loaf on the table. “A man with intellect still weakens without this.”

“Yes. Scripture says that man does not live by bread alone, but by the utterance of God within the bread. The food carries a Divine life-force. The question is not merely how wheat feeds flesh, but why a human soul receives vitality through something created earlier and apparently lower.”

Nadav said, “Because the body is an animal too.”

“That does not reach the point the source is making. The ordering itself hints that what stands lower in the visible chain may contain a root from which the eater receives.”

Baruch remembered the mine. The newest tunnel sometimes depended on a buried seam laid down long before the settlement existed. Haggai allowed the image but kept it modest. “Do not make geology into theology. Only learn not to judge root by surface order.”

The missing wagon arrived at dusk. Uri baked through the night, and the smell drew exhausted workers toward the ovens.

Nadav took his allotted loaf and turned it over in his hands. Yesterday it had been grain. Before man, the teaching said, plants and animals already stood in creation. Yet now the last-created man depended on what preceded him, and the verse directed attention beyond bread’s crust to the Divine utterance by which it lived.
