# Brick by Brick the Arrows Spent

When a retaining wall failed after rain, Tal-Or worked through the night making emergency bricks. Nadav complained that the labor was senseless: mud pressed, lifted, carried, set, and then repeated until every back ached.

Haggai let him complain before speaking. “The source describes Egypt in the same language of crushing work—mortar and bricks. Yet it teaches that the labor itself participated in refinement.”

Seth looked uneasy. “How can the acts of oppressors refine those they oppress?”

“Because evil expends its force through the evil it performs. The teaching compares this to arrows: ‘I will spend My arrows upon them.’ Once an arrow has flown, that particular force is spent. The oppression does not become good; its power is consumed in its own expression while Israel passes through the furnace.”

Nadav wanted a simpler story in which every cruel act produced an immediate visible gain. Haggai refused it. “Refinement is not permission for cruelty, and suffering is not called pleasant. The source is describing what happened to the force of impurity through the bondage.”

Baruch pressed another brick into the wooden frame. “So the mortar and brick are named because the work was hard and material, not because bricks are spiritually evil.”

“Correct.”

By dawn the emergency wall stood. Tal-Or’s labor had been voluntary and protective, nothing like Egypt; Haggai insisted on the difference. The wall was only a place to hold the image of force being spent through repeated action.

Nadav flexed his blistered hands. A night of chosen work had exhausted him. He could barely imagine generations under compulsion.

The source’s claim remained severe: the furnace did not merely delay Sinai. Through bitter labor the obstructing force was used up, until a people could emerge prepared for the Torah that would enter the physical world.
