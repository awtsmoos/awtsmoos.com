# The Torch Returned to the Shaft

A pocket of gas ignited in the western mine and sent a tongue of flame racing upward through an empty vent. The men escaped, but Baruch stood outside staring at the fire’s climb.

“It never asks permission to rise,” he said.

Haggai answered, “That is why the teaching uses fire for the movement called *ratzo*.”

The next day he took the miners back only as far as the safe chamber. There he taught that the living creatures of the prophetic vision run and return. When a created being perceives that its source is without boundary, longing can flare like a torch—to be consumed in its source rather than remain a separate thing.

Uri asked, “Then why return?”

Haggai poured water from a skin into a basin. “Because the same service contains *shov*. ‘If your heart runs, return to One.’ Wisdom is compared to water, and wisdom is *koach mah*—the power of ‘what,’ the humility in which self-importance gives way.”

Nadav had expected the highest man to be the one who never came back down. “So returning to the workbench is not failure?”

“Not when the return is to One. Running and returning both come from Him.”

Caleb reopened the shaft under strict limits. Baruch descended first, not because his yearning had cooled but because men still needed the tunnel shored and the trapped tools recovered. Above, the burned vent remained black. Below, water from the seep ran steadily along the trench.

By evening the mine was stable. Baruch emerged covered in dust and sat beside the basin.

Haggai did not praise him for choosing earth over heaven. That would have falsified the lesson. He praised the joining: the flame that knew how to rise, and the humility that knew how to return.
