# The Peace Higher Than Both

Uri and Baruch quarreled over a new smelting channel. Uri wanted more water near the furnace. Baruch warned that too much would kill the heat. Each man defended the element he understood.

Haggai drew them back to the verse already named in the teaching: “He makes peace in His heights.”

“Fire and water oppose one another when each stands in its own nature,” he said. “Their peace does not come because fire secretly becomes water or water agrees to burn. It comes from a level higher than both, before which both are null and within which both can be joined.”

Nadav saw an opportunity. “Then compromise. Half the fire and half the water.”

“That is not the teaching.”

Haggai explained that the higher peace is not a negotiated middle. A source above the two opposites contains and subordinates both. From there their contradiction no longer rules them. This is why the source can return to *ratzo* and *shov* after speaking of physical Torah: upward thirst and downward drawing can coexist because both are rooted in what transcends their opposition.

Baruch reduced the channel for practical reasons, and Uri moved the trough. The arrangement worked, but Haggai refused to make the engineering result proof of the doctrine. “A furnace is only a picture. The teaching concerns the spiritual source from which opposite movements receive.”

At dusk Caleb sounded the old shofar by the gate. Its note crossed the yard where flame still rose and water still ran.

“Now,” Haggai said, “we are ready to understand why the shofar belongs at the head of this teaching. It does not merely add another sound. It points toward the place from which the opposites can be held together.”

The men stopped arguing about which element was higher. The question had moved above both of them.
