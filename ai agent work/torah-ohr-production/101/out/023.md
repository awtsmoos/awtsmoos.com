# A Voice Is a Drawing

Seth had copied the word “voices” many times and finally asked what a voice itself added to the teaching. Haggai answered by making him speak across the council room.

“Before you spoke,” Haggai said, “the breath and intention were hidden within you. Then the voice carried what was inward outward toward articulated words.”

He was careful with the comparison. The source does not make the Divine like a human body. It uses physical voice as an image of *hamshachah*—a drawing from concealment into revelation. Breath comes from within and becomes audible; so, by analogy only, the Torah’s ‘voice’ indicates a revelation drawn from what is hidden.

Nadav said, “Then every sound is revelation.”

“Not in the sense we mean. We are defining the image chosen by the teaching. At Sinai, the voices signify Divine drawings—revelations from the Infinite into a form that can reach below.”

Baruch remembered the shofar. “Its blast was also a voice.”

“Yes, but the source has been distinguishing the source of the shofar from the later voices. The shofar points higher, while the voices are the channels of drawing that emerge.”

Seth tried the lesson by whispering, then speaking aloud. The difference was not truth versus falsehood; the same thought could remain inward or cross into expression.

That afternoon the council announced a change in mine safety. The rule had existed in Seth’s draft before anyone heard it, but only when Caleb proclaimed it at the gate did it become audible to the workers. Haggai again refused to equate a town decree with Divine revelation.

Still, Seth understood the source’s word better. A ‘voice’ was not merely noise around Sinai. It marked movement: what had been concealed being drawn forth into revelation.
