# The Great Wheel Was Still a Thing

A storm cleared the sky so completely that Tal-Or’s children could see stars they had never noticed. Nadav pointed upward and said, “Anyone would tremble before things that enormous.”

Haggai replied, “That is exactly the distinction the source makes.”

A finite soul can be moved by the Infinite precisely because the Infinite is beyond the soul’s thought and measure. Israel’s response at Sinai came from this encounter with what no created magnitude can contain. The movement was not simply awe before something very large.

The *Erev Rav*, by contrast, were impressed by the visible grandeur of created things—the sun, moon, stars, and heavenly order. Such beings may be vast beyond ordinary experience, but they remain created *yesh*, entities with measure and place. Fear before magnitude is not yet bittul before the Infinite Himself.

Nadav pointed to the largest star he could see. “But if something is large enough, does the difference matter?”

“Completely. Add measure to measure forever and you still speak about measured existence. The Infinite is not the largest member of creation.”

Haggai connected this with the later demand of the mixed multitude for “a god that will go before us”—something visible, graspable, placed before their eyes. The desire itself revealed attachment to a form that remained a thing.

Baruch extinguished the yard lamps, and the stars seemed brighter. No one denied their splendor. Haggai insisted only that splendor not be confused with the source beyond all measure.

When the children went inside, Nadav remained under the sky. The great wheel of heaven had once seemed to him the nearest possible image of divinity. Now its very greatness proved the opposite lesson: however immense, it was still something. The voice at Sinai came from beyond the category of something large.
