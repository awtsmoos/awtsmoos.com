# The Test Hidden in Daily Bread

The first pale cakes appeared after a night of silver mist. They lay among the reeds outside Tal-Or, round as small shields and sweet beneath their dust. The settlement had exhausted its grain, and the mine stores held only ore. Men gathered the cakes by handfuls, laughing until Haggai ordered them weighed. Each household received one day’s need.

Nadav, the ration master, asked why no one should store tomorrow’s portion. A law concerning bread seemed trivial beside the charter Haggai promised to teach.

“Because a man who cannot walk through one morning without possessing the next,” Haggai said, “will turn every later command into property.”

Nadav heard insult. He had led Tal-Or through two lean winters by hoarding. His private chamber held dried roots, lamp oil, and grain withheld from public books. He called secrecy prudence.

Uri the baker distributed the cakes. Widows came first, then injured miners, then working crews. Nadav altered the list so council supporters received larger shares. The bread test began before anyone tasted it.

Seth, the young clerk, noticed the changes. Nadav told him leadership required rewarding stable men. Seth copied the false totals.

That evening families ate the seed-bread beside cold ovens. Its sweetness did not resemble wheat. Baruch the ore washer gave half his portion to Uri’s feverish daughter, though he had worked waist-deep in water all day. Nadav watched from the storehouse door. The uncommanded gift troubled him more than theft; it revealed a law inside the people that his allocations did not create.

He carried six extra cakes into his private chamber and sealed the door. If they remained sound, he would prove daily dependence unnecessary. If they spoiled, he would conceal the result.

Before dawn, a smell of decay filled the corridor. Worms pushed through the basket weave. Nadav buried the cakes beneath slag and announced that every portion had been consumed correctly.

The bread had arrived before the full charter because it examined the vessel that would receive law. Tal-Or could memorize every command and still remain enslaved to fear. Nadav passed the first test publicly and failed it alone.
