# Beyond the Chain of Channels

The survey took seven days. By the eighth, Mattan had drawn Giv’at Or’s water system on a sheet of scraped hide large enough to cover Avram’s council table.

The upper spring fed a settling pool. The pool fed two reservoirs. The reservoirs fed six gates. The gates fed neighborhood pipes, and the pipes fed household cisterns. Every descent narrowed the flow and added another keeper, another rule, another chance for obstruction. It was a sensible system. That was precisely why Avram son of Netanel trusted it.

Mattan placed a charcoal mark outside the diagram.

“What is that?” Avram asked.

“The old quarry shaft.”

“It is not part of the waterworks.”

“Not yet.”

Below the spring, before the settling pool, an abandoned stone tunnel passed through the hill and emerged above the lower quarter. Mattan proposed lining it with fired clay. The new conduit would not replace the normal channels; it would provide a direct descent for emergencies and for the lowest yards whose pressure vanished whenever the upper districts drew heavily.

Avram refused before Mattan finished speaking.

“No unmeasured line leaves the spring.”

“It can be measured at the mouth.”

“No line bypasses the trust’s gates.”

“That is why it would work when the gates fail.”

Their voices carried into the courtyard. Leah stopped grinding willow bark. Nechemiah, who had been reading beneath the fig tree, closed his book.

He asked Avram to explain the difference between Abraham and Moses in the teaching they had been studying.

Avram resented being examined in front of Mattan. Still, he answered. Abraham was chesed, kindness, drawing revelation into the world through love, hospitality, faith, and movement of the heart. Moses drew from another height.

“What height?” Nechemiah asked.

“Beyond the ordinary chain of descent.”

“And what follows from that?”

Avram looked again at the map.

Within a chain, distance mattered. The spring was purer than a cistern; a spoken order was subtler than a clay valve; thought was farther from mud than mud was from thought. Each level emerged through a graded transformation from what preceded it. But the text they were learning said that above the chain, the comparison itself failed. Spiritual and material were both equally without proportion to the Infinite. If neither had a claim of closeness, then what was utterly beyond both could reach the lowest no less truly than the highest.

Mattan tapped the charcoal mark beside the map. “I am not saying this tunnel is that teaching.”

Nechemiah nodded sharply. “Good. Stone is stone. Do not turn plumbing into God.”

“But,” Mattan continued, “the town has made hierarchy into a superstition. We think whatever passes through more upper offices must be holier because it remained farther from the clay yards.”

Leah set down the pestle. “My patients live in the clay yards.”

Avram walked to the window. From the spring house he could see roofs stepping down the hill: academy tiles, merchants’ courtyards, dye sheds, then the smoke of the kilns. For years he had understood generosity as opening the top and letting benefit descend through every authorized level. He had never asked whether his love of ordered descent had become love of being indispensable to it.

Nechemiah spoke more quietly. “Moses is described through מים and through מה—water and self-nullification. Not because water is the Infinite, and not because a man becomes nothing in a theatrical sense. The point is that bittul does not insist on itself as the measure of what can be revealed. Therefore the drawing can reach action itself.”

Avram returned to the table.

He did not approve the bypass. Not yet. But he stopped calling it unlawful and asked Mattan for pressure calculations, clay requirements, labor days, and an inspection plan.

It was less dramatic than surrender, and more difficult. A man can donate a hundred jars while remaining master of every jar. To consider a channel that might continue serving the lower quarter when his own gates were closed required Avram to imagine blessing arriving without confirming his centrality.

Mattan gathered the hide map.

At the door Avram said, “Bring me the figures tomorrow.”

For the first time, the line beyond the chain remained on the plan.
