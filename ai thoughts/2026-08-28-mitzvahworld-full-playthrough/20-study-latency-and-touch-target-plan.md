# Study Latency and Touch-Target Closure

The Awtsmoos gives the traveler one intention and one world, while Awtsmoos.com must not turn that intention into hundreds of cold requests before a foot can move in the field.

## Direct browser evidence

- Virgin menu: 3.18 seconds, zero exceptions, zero console errors.
- Study to visible 1440×615 WebGL gameplay: about 33.0 seconds cold.
- The delay is dominated by a broad raw-module waterfall, especially procedural-core modules taking roughly 1.4–2.1 seconds each.
- The existing generated `world` chunk is post-play world-system installation, not the playable foundation/core boundary.
- `EretzStagedRuntime` deliberately selects raw responsive module URLs for `EretzWorldFoundation` and `BootstrapCoreRuntimeAssembly`.
- Desktop HUD scaling makes a nominal 48px retract control render at 42px.

## Chosen direction pending measured bundle sizes

1. Measure foundation and core as independent CompactJS candidates rather than creating one giant playable monolith.
2. If each compressed boundary is reasonable, publish them as explicit generated runtime chunks and make `EretzStagedRuntime` import those terminal artifacts.
3. Preserve dynamic seams inside the heavier graph where they protect distant/optional/creator systems.
4. Keep presentation, post-play world systems, optional quality, creator, and distant-region systems deferred beyond playable canvas.
5. Remove CSS scaling from the interactive HUD shell and preserve real 48px rail/retract hit targets.
6. Rebuild canonically, then repeat a virgin target cache-disabled menu + Study audit and compare request count, click-to-canvas, long tasks, errors, and geometry.

## Release gate

No push/deploy until cold Study is materially faster, gameplay remains error-free, primary controls are at least 48px, Creator works through Place/Undo/Redo/Save/Restore/Remix, and desktop/mobile browser gates are green.
