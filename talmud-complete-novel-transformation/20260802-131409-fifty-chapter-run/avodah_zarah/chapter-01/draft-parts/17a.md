### Page Marker — Avodah Zarah 17a

## A Teaching From the Entrance of Heresy

Yaakov of Kefar Sekhanya had asked whether payment received through prostitution could build a bathroom for the High Priest.

He cited a teaching attributed to Jesus: money gathered from filth may return to a place of filth.

Rabbi Eliezer enjoyed the ingenuity. He later understood his arrest as punishment for approaching the entrance of heresy and taking pleasure in its words.

The Proverbs warning to keep far from “her” is read both as distance from heresy and distance from oppressive government.

## The Brothel and the Idol House

The source compares the entrances of sexual transgression and idolatry. Each can seize a person whose path approaches too near.

One should not rely on strength developed only after entering the danger.

## Elazar Ben Durdayya

Elazar ben Durdayya visited every prostitute he could find.

One woman told him that his repentance was as impossible as a breath returning to its source. The sentence broke the illusion that he could continue without final consequence.

He asked mountains and hills, heaven and earth, sun and moon, stars and constellations to seek mercy for him. Each answered that it must seek mercy for itself.

He concluded that the matter depended on no one but himself.

He placed his head between his knees, wept until his soul left, and a Divine Voice called him “Rabbi Elazar ben Durdayya” and destined him for the World-to-Come.

Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi wept: one person acquires that world through many years, another in one hour; penitents are not merely accepted but can be called Rabbi.

## The Two Roads

Rabbi Ḥanina and Rabbi Yonatan reached two paths, one passing an idol house and one a brothel.

One preferred the idol path because the idolatrous inclination had been weakened. The other chose the brothel path to overcome temptation and receive reward.

That encounter crosses into 17b, where Torah itself becomes their guard.
