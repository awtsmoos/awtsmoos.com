Yackov, Leah and Rachel (יעקב, לאה ורחל)

Core definition

In the corpus's kabbalistic reading, the Patriarch Yackov and his wives Leah and Rachel are not only historical figures but a map of the divine structure: Yackov is the central, joining partzuf (Tiferes / Ze'ir Anpin, "the middle bar"), whose two unions — with the higher Nukva, Leah, and the lower Nukva, Rachel — correspond to the two modes of the feminine principle: thought and speech, the hidden and the revealed [19803/maamar_003 :: §575; 15879/maamar_006 :: §683].

How the Rebbeim develop it

Yackov — "the middle bar from end to end." The verse "the middle bar that extends from end to end" (Shemos 26:28) is read as Yackov's office: the erector of Malchus. Malchus is the true ayin of Atzilus — essential bitul, a concealed point — and its rebuilding into a partzuf requires the highest light, "the inward, essential light of the Ohr Ein Sof," which Yackov draws from the inwardness of Keser for "the expansion of this point" [19803/maamar_003 :: §575, Summary]. One maamar reads Yackov's journey "to Charan, to Lavan" as the descent to clarify the sparks — "clarifying there Leah and Rachel's aspect" — paralleling the soul's descent into the body to clarify and fix the animal soul [31633/maamar_021 :: §305].

Leah — the higher Nukva, thought. Leah is the great hei — Binah: "Binah is 'mother of the children': Leah (the great hei = Binah) bore six sons — the traits are born per the intellect" [31608/maamar_004 :: §255]. In the Zohar's "mi bara eileh," "mi" is Binah — Leah, "the great hei of machshavah" (thought), mother of the six middos [58841/maamar_006 :: Study guide]. Hence "Leah and Rachel are machshava (thought) and dibbur (speech)" respectively [15879/maamar_006 :: §683]. "V'shem hagedolah Leah, v'shem haketanah Rachel" — the greater one's name was Leah: Leah's level is higher, the hidden thought-world [31628/maamar_003 :: §4623].

Rachel — the lower Nukva, speech. Rachel is Malchus as revealed speech — the lower world of the revealed. In the Purim maamar's historical typology, Knesses Yisroel in the Beis HaMikdash era was Rachel — "the revealed world," beautiful of form and beautiful of appearance — while in the Achashverosh exile she is Esther, hiddenness [60801/maamar_022 :: Overview]. In the soul's map, the animal soul is "Esther" when hidden and "Rachel" when revealed — the Divine soul's revealed counterpart in the world of action [19803/maamar_003 :: Summary §1393].

Key distinctions

Leah vs. Rachel (thought vs. speech). Thought is the inner garment — the seat of essential hiskashrus, where connection with Divinity is essential; speech (and action) can proceed without hiskashrus at all. Therefore prayer, whose essence is hiskashrus, is "thought and intention's aspect" — the speech of prayer exists to arouse the intention [31633/maamar_021 :: §454]. This is the spiritual meaning of Leah's precedence: the hidden, greater level.

The two unions. Yackov's two marriages map the two directions of divine union: with Leah, the union of Tiferes with Binah (the higher mother, in the world of thought); with Rachel, the union of Ze'ir Anpin with the lower Nukva, Malchus — the union that produces revelation in the lower worlds, and which Yackov must "erect" by drawing Keser's inward light.

Charan as the unshining speech. "Charan" is read as "charon" (wrath) / the dried throat: the state where voice does not shine in speech — the descent Yackov undertakes precisely to clarify Leah and Rachel, the sparks fallen into nogah [31633/maamar_021 :: §454].

Related concepts

  • Ze'ir Anpin / Tiferes — the masculine partzuf Yackov embodies.
  • Malchus — the Nukva Yackov builds; "Rachel" in its revealed mode, "Esther" in its hidden mode.
  • Binah — the great hei, Leah's root as mother of the middos.
  • Machshavah and dibbur — thought and speech as the soul's inner and outer garments.
