Gevurah as judgment (גבורה בדין) — the attribute of judgment

Core definition

Gevurah — the second of the emotive sefiros, the left line's counterpart to Chesed — is the attribute of judgment (din). "The blow is from the Elokim name — gevurah and judgment" [19801/maamar_019 :: עב׳ 329]. All Divine judgment flows from this middah: "it is known that all judgment and punishment is from the aspect of the judgments and contractions of the achorayim (the hinder-part) of the name Elokim, which is the aspect of the attribute of judgment" [31663/maamar_002 :: עב׳ 229–233]. While Chesed bestows without precision, "the judgment's middah" is "completely opposing chesed's middah": it measures, weighs, and withholds — "the middah of chesed is not precise at all," but judgment is exact [31654/maamar_012 :: עמוד תפב].

How the Rebbeim develop it

Elokim and the achorayim. The Name Havayah is chesed — mercy; the Name Elokim is gevurah — judgment. "Wherever it says 'and Havayah' it means He and His court: the court is judgment/gevurah, 'He' is chesed" [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ז)]. In the ten plagues, "their beginning was judgment and gevurah, and their end chesed and mercy": the plagues came from Elokim's gevurah, and "afterwards the judgments were sweetened with the chasodim of the name Havayah, through Moshe's prayer." Pharaoh — who "knew only Elokim, not Havayah" — thereby saw "that the aspect of the chesed of the name Havayah is greater, and its power more abundant, than the gevurah of the name Elokim," until "the gevurah of the name Elokim is nullified" [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ח)]. This is the meaning of "Havayah, He is Elokim" — the unification of the two Names [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ח)].

Harsh judgment. The plague of the firstborn was different: "for in the plague of the firstborn there was for Egypt harsh judgment entirely" [31663/maamar_002 :: עב׳ 140] — "from the aspect of the gevurah of the name Havayah itself": there "was a division of the chesed and the gevurah within Havayah itself — the gevurah extended to Egypt and the chasodim of Havayah to Israel" [31663/maamar_002 :: עב׳ 343–349]. This is why "at the plague of the firstborn, chesed and gevurah ruled together" — at that hour there were two opposite actions at once: "Egypt smitten, Israel saved," as in "and Havayah shall smite Egypt, smiting and healing" — smiting for Egypt, healing for Israel [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ז)].

The attribute of judgment as prosecutor. The middas hadin (attribute of judgment) prosecutes even the righteous: "as the attribute of judgment argued even at the time of the splitting of the Sea: 'these [worship idols], etc., and these [worship idols],'" — Israel and Egypt alike unworthy, in strict judgment's eyes [31663/maamar_002 :: עב׳ 416].

Creation in judgment. "At first it arose to create with judgment" — the world was to be created with din; only when He saw the world could not stand did He join mercy [58837/maamar_013 :: Chunk 2 summary]. So gevurah too is a kind of drawing — the drawing of measure and limit, without which no world could receive.

Yitzchak and Gavriel. Yitzchak is "the gevurah's sefirah" — "the fear of Yitzchak was to me" — the patriarch of judgment and awe, opposite to Yackov's chesed [19801/maamar_019 :: Summary]. The angels: Michoel (water) is chesed, Gavriel (fire) is gevurah — "the fire's minister, who is gevurah" [19801/maamar_019 :: Intro]. In the sefiros' arrangement, "the sefiros Binah, gevurah, hod in the left line" — "gevurah the Binah's branch" [19801/maamar_019 :: Summary].

Key distinctions

Judgment vs. restraint. Gevurah has two faces: as din it judges and punishes — "the blow is from the Elokim name" — while as restraint (tzimtzum) it conceals and limits the light, making reception possible (see Gevurah as restraint). Both are the same middah's two modes: precision toward the recipient, contraction toward the light.

Elokim vs. Havayah. "Elokim" is gevurah — judgment, the court; "Havayah" is chesed — mercy, "He" Himself. Judgment comes from Elokim's achorayim; when Havayah's chesed shines, "the gevurah of the name Elokim is nullified" [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ח)].

The plagues vs. the firstborn. The other plagues began in judgment/gevurah and ended in chesed/mercy through Moshe's prayer; the firstborn's plague was harsh judgment with no return to chesed — "from the gevurah of Havayah itself" [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ח)].

Related concepts

  • Gevurah as restraint — the same middah in its concealing, measuring mode
  • Chesed — gevurah's opposite and the sweetener of its judgments
  • Tiferes — Yackov, who "makes peace" between chesed and gevurah
  • Elokim — the Divine Name of judgment; its achorayim the source of punishments
  • Yitzchak — the patriarch of gevurah; "the fear of Yitzchak"
