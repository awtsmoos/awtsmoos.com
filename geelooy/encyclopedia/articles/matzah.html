Matzah (מצה) — the Unleavened Bread

Core definition

Matzah is the unleavened bread eaten at the Pesach seder, "the bread of affliction" (לחם עוני) "without taste at all" — the aspect of nullification and lowliness (bitul and shiflus), the opposite of chametz, which is "in raising and haughtiness specifically" [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 533–535]. It is called meichla d'meheimnusa — "the food of faith" — "the food strengthening the faith... above reason and knowledge specifically" [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 536–539].

How the Rebbeim develop it

The difference between matzah and chametz is in their letters: matzah with a hei, chametz with a ches [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 540–541]. The hei refers to the three worlds of Beriah, Yetzirah, Asiyah: the hei has three lines — two connected lines indicating Beriah and Yetzirah (in Yetzirah too much light still shining, "the host of heaven bows to You"), and only Asiyah is as the aspect of yesh — the third leg severed [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 542–550]. The ches too is three lines — only all three lines are connected — referring to the three worlds of kelipah, the three impure kelipos, impure completely; therefore the ches indicates sin (chet) [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 551–556].

Matzah is "a vessel for the revelation of the aspect and level of the King of kings of kings, the Holy One, blessed be He — from being in the aspect of nullification and lowliness — 'the bread of affliction' specifically" [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 557–558]. Precisely because it has no taste and no selfhood, it can receive the highest revelation.

Yet matzah carries within it another meaning — the language of "strife and quarrel" (matzah u'merivah). "The truth being that the matzah is the aspect of nullification and lowliness; but it is only the nullification of the yesh: the yesh still in its existence, only nullified for the hour — not that the yesh was nullified completely" [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 559–563]. Matzah effects the nullification in the yesh — it subdues the ego without erasing it — "this being that matzah is the language of 'strife and quarrel'; effecting the nullification in the yesh; therefore needing guarding — as it is written, 'you shall guard the matzos'" [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 564–566]. The strife is the inner struggle: the yesh remains, and the matzah must continually nullify it — hence the guarding.

The redemption from Mitzrayim was likened to a lamp: "the lamp only shining the darkness — not transforming the darkness's essence to light literally — only shining in the place it shines; not like the day's light, which transforms the darkness, driving it out" [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 567–572]. So the matzah effects the nullification in the yesh — it shines the darkness but does not transform it. "It is a guarded night" (Shemos 12:42): the yesh still in its perfection, and the guarding is for the yesh's nullification [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 574–576].

In avodah this is "nullify your will before His will" [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 577]. One reaches "nullify your will" — the yesh's nullification — through the maror specifically: the maror in the seder plate's order is in the middle line, the aspect of mercies — "effecting the many mercies, to be in the aspect 'nullify your will' — the yesh's nullification" [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 578–580]. The matzah's lowliness and the maror's bitterness together produce the nullification of the self.

Key distinctions

  • Matzah vs. chametz: matzah — bitul and lowliness, the bread of affliction without taste; chametz — raising and haughtiness. In the letters: the hei's third leg severed (Asiyah as yesh, apart) vs. the ches's three connected lines (the three impure kelipos) [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 533–556].
  • Matzah as faith vs. matzah as strife: meichla d'meheimnusa — the food strengthening faith above reason — yet matzah u'merivah — strife: it nullifies the yesh "for the hour," not completely [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 536–566].
  • Lamp vs. day: the exodus's redemption is a lamp — shining the darkness, not transforming its essence — which is why the guarding is needed [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 567–576].
  • Matzah vs. maror: the matzah is the lowliness itself; the maror (bitterness, middle line, mercies) is the means to reach "nullify your will" [15962/maamar_012 :: עב׳ 578–580].

Related concepts

Chametz, Pesach, the exodus from Mitzrayim, Bitul (nullification), Meichla d'meheimnusa (food of faith), Maror, "You shall guard the matzos."
