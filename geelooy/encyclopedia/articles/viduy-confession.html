Viduy (וידוי) — Confession

Core definition

Viduy — confession of sins before Havayah — is a distinct mitzvah and a central component of teshuvah. The corpus focuses on viduy devarim, the verbal confession: "the confession's letters: saying and mentioning the sin in speech" [31633/maamar_022 :: §311]. Its power: "Taking all the vitality utterly: not even a trace of vitality remaining in the evil" [31633/maamar_022 :: §308–309].

How the Rebbeim develop it

The whispered viduy. On Rosh Hashanah, during the tekios, there is "the viduy (confession) in a whisper": "that which is very hard for him — to change his nature in many matters" — "and the matter of the viduy in a whisper: to change in his very essence" [15720/maamar_005 :: §286–299]. The whispered confession "overturns 'from this vessel to this vessel,' changing one's nature from essence to essence — becoming entirely good" — and thereby Malchus becomes a vessel for the supernal light [15720/maamar_005 :: Summary p. 30].

Speech reaches the depth. "The verbal confession reaches more in the soul's inwardness and depth... Speech arouses more" [31633/maamar_022 :: §312–313]. Teshuvah itself is "from the heart and soul's depth and inwardness," and "through the confession's speech, his soul's essence is aroused more — till taking all the vitality from the evil, nothing remaining" [31633/maamar_022 :: §320–323]. The mechanism: the sin drew vitality into the evil's domain; naming it in speech reclaims the "letters" — the vitality is drawn back out, leaving nothing.

The tears of confession. "This is the tears: coming specifically through speech. Through the confession's speech, his soul's essence aroused more. Till taking all the vitality from the evil — nothing remaining" [31633/maamar_022 :: Summary, tail]. Speech is the channel through which the heart's depth breaks into tears.

The great hand. Viduy belongs to the Ne'ilah prayer's "great hand": "You give a hand to the transgressors, Your right hand outstretched to receive the returners" — "the outstretched hand's aspect... raising also from the evil's bottom and depth. This is drawn on the verbal confession. The fixing in completion: nothing remaining" [31633/maamar_022 :: Summary, tail]. The same "great hand" logic explains Yackov's gift to Esav: lest he took the blessings not by law, he sent a gift "as the sent goat's matter — not accusing, turned to a defender" [31633/maamar_022 :: Summary, tail].

Two levels of teshuvah. The viduy-discourse situates confession within teshuvah's two levels: teshuvah tata'ah — the bitterness on his distance from Hashem, "especially on the sin," regretting; and teshuvah ila'ah — "and the spirit shall return to the Elokim who gave it," in the soul's essence [31633/maamar_022 :: Summary §828]. The verbal confession serves both: it begins in regret over the specific sin and reaches the soul's essence.

Key distinctions

Viduy vs. teshuvah's other parts. Teshuvah comprises abandoning the sin, regret, and confession; viduy is the speech-act that completes the reclaiming — "the fixing in completion: nothing remaining" [31633/maamar_022 :: Summary, tail].

Thought vs. speech. The confession must be verbal ("viduy devarim") precisely because speech arouses more and reaches deeper into the soul's inwardness than thought alone [31633/maamar_022 :: §311–313].

The letters reclaimed. The sin's "letters" — the vitality invested in the evil act — are "taken" through being said and mentioned in speech [31633/maamar_022 :: §311].

Related concepts

  • Teshuvah — return; viduy is its completing speech-act; teshuvah tata'ah and teshuvah ila'ah.
  • Ne'ilah — the closing prayer whose "great hand" raises even from the evil's depth.
  • Charatah — regret; the bitterness on distance that precedes and accompanies confession.
  • Cheshbon — the soul's accounting; "the holiness standing in cheshbon" vs. not.
