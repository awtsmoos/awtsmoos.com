Tefillas HaDerech (תפלת הדרך) — The Traveler's Prayer

Core definition

Tefillas HaDerech — the traveler's prayer, and in the corpus specifically the short prayer of desert travelers (Mishnah Berachos 4) — is expounded as a prayer for the exile itself: "u'vechol parashat ha'ibur yihyu tzorkeihem lefanecha" ("and in every 'parshat ha'ibur,' may their needs be before You"). The Talmud asks what "parshat ha'ibur" means — and the maamar reads it as exile-as-pregnancy, praying that even in exile Israel's needs be met from innerness [15911/maamar_006 :: Overview; 15911/maamar_006 :: phr. 23].

How the Rebbeim develop it

The prayer's key phrase is "bechol parashat ha'ibur": the analogy to a pregnant woman (ishah ubarah). Exile is ibur — Ze'ir Anpin's pregnancy in Imma's womb, "his head between his knees," utter smallness. During this gestation, bounty flows not through Ze'ir Anpin but from Imma's makifim, where "k'chashicha k'orah" — small and great are equal — and even the chitzonim receive (Binah's free bounty, without mitzvos). Redemption will be Ze'ir Anpin's birth: transmission passing through Ze'ir Anpin, whose Chesed and Gevurah limit nearness and distance so bounty flows only "to the fitting place" [15911/maamar_006 :: Overview].

The Talmud's gloss sharpens the plea: "even at a time when You are filled with wrath upon them, as it were — nevertheless, 'may their needs be before You'" [15911/maamar_006 :: phr. 23]. The prayer's essence is distilled in the maamar's own words: "that even at this time, 'yihyu tzorkeihem lefanecha' — their needs be before You — from the aspect of innerness" [15911/maamar_006 :: phr. 405]. Hence the traveler's prayer: even in exile — even in the state of wrath and concealment — may Israel's needs be met from innerness: the Name's letters with their milui (filling), the inwardness of transmission rather than its mere externality [15911/maamar_006 :: Overview; 15911/maamar_006 :: phr. 706].

The maamar closes with birth's sequel — yenikah, suckling Imma's milk (the sovev drawn into Ze'ir Anpin) — and the inner mechanics of the prayer's answer: milk comes from blood; in avodah, blood is "rishpei eish shalheves Kah" — love's fiery thirst from contemplating the worlds' distance — answered by supernal love that refines it; then blood settles into milk — gevuros sweetened into supernal chasadim, ahavah b'ta'anugim — when the Divine light shines revealed in the soul, effecting utter bitul [15911/maamar_006 :: phr. 706].

The maamar also answers the hard question behind the prayer: the chitzonim have no vessels — how does makif-light become their vitality at all? The answer is the Mishnah's "hakite'a yotzei b'kav shelo" — the amputee's wooden kav: not the true leg (NeH"Y itself, whose milu'im-with-names carry holiness's transmission) but the milu'im alone ("kav charuvin" — dry carobs; achorayim), like hair to intellect, the vegetative to the speaker. Through these the transmission changes into utter materialization — fit for the chitzonim [15911/maamar_006 :: Study guide]. The traveler's prayer thus operates inside this economy: even in exile, may Israel's portion come from the inner letters with their milui, not from the externalized achorayim that feed the other side [15911/maamar_006 :: Overview].

Key distinctions

  • Ibur vs. leidah. The prayer is said from within the pregnancy of exile — before the birth of redemption, when bounty comes only from the makifim [15911/maamar_006 :: Overview].
  • Innerness vs. externality. The prayer's request is not merely for needs to be met, but for them to be met from innerness — the letters with their milui [15911/maamar_006 :: phr. 706].
  • Blood vs. milk. Fiery love (blood) refined and settled into sweetened chasadim (milk) — the transformation the prayer seeks [15911/maamar_006 :: phr. 706].

Related concepts

Ze'ir Anpin, Galus and Geulah, Makifim, the Milui of the Divine Name, Yenikah.
