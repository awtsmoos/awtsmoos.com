Savlanut (סבלנות) — patience, forbearance

Core definition

Savlanut — patience, the bearing (sevel) of yoke and decree — is the soul's capacity to endure what opposes it without inner rebellion. In the corpus's main exposition, on Yeshaya 29:19 ("and the humble shall increase joy in Havayah"), Rashi identifies the anavim (humble) with the savlanim (patient ones): "from this comes patience — bearing yoke and decree — as is the nature of one who is nullified in his essence, who does not reckon with himself at all" [31667/maamar_018 :: p.רלז].

How the Rebbeim develop it

Patience as the fruit of bitul. Patience is not passivity but the outward face of essential nullification. "The humble one," whose subtle faith illuminates everything — "all is comfortable, all is right with him" — is the patient one: because he does not reckon with himself, there is no self to be offended, and the yoke can be borne. "And for the most part he hides among the vessels, distancing himself from every matter and thing of expansion, his being in the nature of restfulness and patience" [31667/maamar_018 :: pp.רלז–רמא].

The patient one's inner knowledge. "The matter of the patient one is that he knows that the bad passes and goes, and the inner good will be revealed — because of which, sometimes even patience will be in joy." This is Rashi's "savlanim" on "and the humble shall increase joy in Havayah": patience too can be joyful, when it sees through the passing bad to the inner good. Dovid is the exemplar: king of Israel, of whom "the halacha is like him everywhere" — yet "I am a worm" (Tehillim 22:7), seeing every person's virtue as above his own. And Hillel the Elder rejoiced at the Simchas Beis HaShoevah with the greatest joy — because he was most humble [31667/maamar_018 :: p.רמא].

The divine "long of patience." The attribute above is erech apayim — "long of patience." The Zohar: "He said to it: is it for nothing that I am called 'long of patience'? — That is: that upon Me it is to pass over first." The Yalkut's "upon Me it is to pass over first" is the divine patience: He passes over sins first, and teshuvah — His own counsel — sweetens all judgments. "For they asked the Holy One, blessed be He — He said: let him do teshuvah, and he shall be atoned for" [60800/maamar_047 :: p.שט].

Patience in teaching and avodah. Yackov Avinu's patience was the condition of his life's work: "to give this to understand — to simple people in general and the dense-minded in particular — only one who is wondrous in patience can." From his will's mighty greatness to publicize His Divinity in the world, Yackov "acquired the good middos until as his birth-nature" — and patience is what let him "continue his service — to explain the Divinity matter also to the simplest" [31681/maamar_012 :: pp.צד–צה].

Patience vs. joy — the deeper unity. The maamar's driving question is how humility (lowliness, non-reckoning with self, restfulness — patience's own nature) and joy (elevation, self-expansion) can meet. The chiddush: humility doesn't merely receive joy, it causes it — being the opposite of hargashas atzmo (self-feeling), which causes sadness. And patience, the humble one's bearing of the yoke, becomes joyful in itself when it knows "the bad passes and the inner good will be revealed" [31667/maamar_018 :: Overview].

Key distinctions

1. Patience vs. passivity — savlanut is not resignation but the bearing of yoke and decree from essential bitul, "not reckoning with oneself at all" [31667/maamar_018 :: p.רלז]. 2. Patience vs. joy — seemingly opposites (restfulness vs. expansion), but "sometimes even patience will be in joy," when it knows the inner good will be revealed [31667/maamar_018 :: p.רמא]. 3. Human patience vs. divine patience — man's patience bears the yoke; Havayah's erech apayim "passes over first," sweetening judgments [60800/maamar_047 :: p.שט]. 4. Patience as teaching-power — only "one who is wondrous in patience" can make Divinity understood even to the simplest; Yackov's example [31681/maamar_012 :: pp.צד–צה].

Related concepts

  • Anavah (ענוה) — humility; Rashi's "anavim = savlanim," the source of patience [31667/maamar_018 :: p.רלז].
  • Bitul (ביטול) — nullification; patience's inner nature — one nullified in essence does not reckon with himself [31667/maamar_018 :: p.רלז].
  • Simchah (שמחה) — joy; what patience, too, can become, knowing the bad passes [31667/maamar_018 :: p.רמא].
  • Erech apayim (ארך אפים) — long of patience; the divine attribute that passes over first [60800/maamar_047 :: p.שט].
