Yiras Shamayim (יראת שמים) — Fear of Heaven

Core definition

Yiras Shamayim — "fear of Heaven" — is the awe and dread of Heaven that the Sages make the precondition of all spiritual attainment: the gate through which one enters to every supernal revelation. Without it, even Torah learning remains locked outside; with it, all levels open. In the corpus it is developed as the outward keys complementing Torah's inward keys, and as the lower fear — the gate-level — inherited from the Patriarchs [19801/maamar_019 :: Summary].

How the Rebbeim develop it

The classic exposition is the Sages' parable (Shabbos 31a): "Every Torah scholar with Torah in him and no fear of Heaven in him is like a treasurer to whom the inward keys were handed but the outward keys were not handed — with what shall he enter?" The Torah is the inward keys' level — "the Torah from Chochmah goes out," the beginning of the inward vessels, in which the Ohr Ein Sof rests. Fear — the lower fear's level — is the outward keys' level [19801/maamar_019 :: Summary].

This is the meaning of the gate: "This is Elokim's gate" — "this is the gate to enter." A gate serves for entrance and exit; to come to all the supernal revelations is through fear specifically. "And now, Israel, what does Havayah your Elokim ask of you but to fear" — and afterward many things are counted, for when there is the gate of fear, one can come to all the levels; therefore it is called a gate, for through it they enter the house [19801/maamar_019 :: Summary].

The gate's very form carries the teaching: the lintel and the two doorposts are the three Patriarchs — Yackov, Yitzchak, Yackov — and in each and every Jew there is this gate in inheritance from the Avos, in the three lines of Torah, avodah, and acts of kindness. Upon this gate-level in every Jew is drawn the level of Chesed and mercies — "and Elokim leapt upon the gate" — a healing to Israel [19801/maamar_019 :: Summary].

The cosmic backdrop is the doctrine of Divine "sleep" and "waking." The Yalkut: when Israel sin, He makes Himself as asleep; when they do His will, "He neither slumbers nor sleeps." Awake above is the revelation of the essential light; asleep, only the externality of the light — expounded through the analogy of man: awake, every faculty acts in revelation each according to its matter; asleep, the faculties withdraw and act in weakness, all equal, opposites joining unordered, as in dreams. Above: sleep is the concealment of the light — not a total removal, for even in exile He renews creation daily — and when the light is concealed it does not shine in the supernal mochin, the souls of Israel; regarding the Ein Sof nothing changes, but below this makes exile [19800/maamar_015 :: Overview]. The revealed faculties root in the concealed faculties and the soul's essence — and fear, as the gate, is what opens the passage from concealment to revelation.

Key distinctions

  • Inward keys vs. outward keys. Torah (from Chochmah, the inward vessels' beginning) vs. fear (the outward keys) — both are needed; Torah alone leaves the treasurer locked out [19801/maamar_019 :: Summary].
  • The gate as entrance and exit. Fear is not only the entry to revelation but its channel of exit — the form through which the Chesed drawn above reaches below, "a healing to Israel" [19801/maamar_019 :: Summary].
  • Asleep vs. awake. The concealment of the light (sleep) vs. its revelation (waking) — fear is the gate that opens from one to the other [19800/maamar_015 :: Overview].

Related concepts

Ahavas Hashem (love of Havayah), Kabbalas Ol (acceptance of the yoke), the Three Avos, Torah (the inward keys), Galus (exile as concealment).
