Gevurah (גבורה) — Strength / Severity

Core definition

Gevurah is the Divine attribute of strength, judgment, and restraint — the sefirah of the "left line," the counterpart and opposite of Chesed (kindness). Where Chesed expands and bestows, Gevurah contracts, limits, measures, and judges: it is the power that says "no," that smites the wicked and withholds bounty from the unworthy. In the corpus it appears both as one of the ten sefiros and as a cosmic force — the "gevurah of the name Elokim," the judgmental aspect of Divinity — whose relationship with Chesed is one of the central dramas of Chassidic theology [31663/maamar_002 :: עב׳ 24].

How the Rebbeim develop it

The paradigm case for Gevurah in the corpus is the plague of the firstborn. At that hour, the maamarim teach, the two attributes of Chesed and Gevurah ruled together: Egypt was smitten and Israel was saved in one moment [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ז)]. This is set against the pattern of the earlier plagues, which came from Elokim — judgment and Gevurah — and were only afterward sweetened into Chesed and mercy, when Pharaoh begged Moshe to pray; Pharaoh, who had known only Elokim, thus learned that the Chesed of the name Havayah is mightier than the Gevurah of the name Elokim, and nullifies it [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ח)]. The plague of the firstborn broke this pattern: it came from the Gevurah of Havayah itself — yet within Havayah, Chesed and Gevurah divided, Gevurah extending to Egypt and the chasodim of Havayah to Israel. A thing and its opposite from one light, in one literal moment — like the future sun taken from its sheath, which will scorch the wicked and heal the righteous, or Sinai's "two-faced" voice, life for Israel and death for the idolaters [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ט)].

How can two complete opposites coexist in one moment? The answer is midnight: the night divides into Gevurah (before midnight) and Chesed (after), and the midnight moment belongs to neither half — it is the memutza (intermediary) that includes both opposites in potential, not in actuality, like the soul-powers as they exist in the soul's essence [31663/maamar_002 :: Overview]. The maamar then traces this intermediary upward: the division of Chesed and Gevurah from Abba and Imma (rooted in Arich Anpin's two mazalos), Keter as the intermediary between Emanator and emanated, Atik above all division — and in Ohr Ein Sof itself, Chesed and Gevurah included without division at all [31663/maamar_002 :: Overview].

A second great exposition takes Iyov 25:2 — "the ruler and the fear with Him, He makes peace in His heights": Michoel (water, Chesed) and Gavriel (fire, Gevurah) — opposites by nature, yet kneaded together, the water not extinguishing the fire — are joined through a supernal light's revelation, higher than both [19801/maamar_019 :: Overview]. The Zohar's reading maps this onto the Patriarchs: "the ruler" is Yackov (Chesed), "the fear" is Yitzchak (Gevurah), and "He makes peace in His heights" is Yackov — Tiferes, "the middle bar passing from end to end," which holds this and this [19801/maamar_019 :: Overview]. The sefiros are seated in three lines: Chochmah, Chesed, Netzach on the right (Chesed the branch of Chochmah); Binah, Gevurah, Hod on the left (Gevurah the branch of Binah); Tiferes in the middle line, rising to Keter, its bestowal in Malchus including right and left [19801/maamar_019 :: Overview].

The reach of Gevurah extends even into nature: sweet fruits root in the Chesed of Asiyah, sour fruits in the Gevurah of Asiyah — the sefiros as the ultimate sources from which, through descent after descent, physical taste unfolds [19801/maamar_019 :: Overview].

Key distinctions

  • Gevurah of Elokim vs. Gevurah of Havayah. The earlier plagues came from Elokim's Gevurah (judgment, then sweetened to Chesed); the plague of the firstborn came from the Gevurah of Havayah itself — harsh judgment with no return to Chesed, "by Havayah's own accord" [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ח)].
  • Successive vs. simultaneous. In the earlier plagues, judgment and healing were sequential (plague, then healing through prayer); at the firstborn, smiting and healing were in one moment — possible only through the intermediary that includes opposites in potential [31663/maamar_002 :: Overview].
  • Sweetened Gevurah vs. harsh Gevurah. The judgments of Elokim were sweetened by Havayah's chasodim through Moshe's prayer; the firstborn's judgment was not sweetened at all [31663/maamar_002 :: Summary (page ח)].

Related concepts

Chesed (kindness), Tiferes (harmony — the middle line that joins them), Elokim (the Divine name of judgment), the Memutza (intermediary), Hamtakah (sweetening of judgments), Makkas Bechoros.
