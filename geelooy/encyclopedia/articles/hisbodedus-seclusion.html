Hisbodedus (התבודדות) — Seclusion / Solitude

Core definition

Hisbodedus is the practice and state of seclusion — withdrawing from company and from the world's disturbance into solitude with oneself and with Divinity. In the corpus it appears in two registers: as a personal avodah-mode (solitude for deep contemplation), and as a historical mode of the tzaddikim (periods of hiddenness before revelation) [31641/hosafa_3 :: §61; 58841/maamar_009 :: §630].

How the Rebbeim develop it

The Shevatim's choice. The twelve tribes "chose for themselves to be in deserts and hisbodedus — not to be disturbed and not confused from their avodah" [31628/maamar_016 :: §3785]. The reason given is exact: the Shevatim's bitul was only the "yesh's" bitul — unlike the Avos' bitul b'metzius — so they needed the desert's seclusion to guard their avodah of clarifying the sparks ("hinei anachnu m'almim alumim basadeh") [31628/maamar_016 :: Summary p. 188; 31678/maamar_018 :: §132].

The ten hidden years. The Rashab's decade between the Maharash's passing (13 Tishrei 5643) and his acceptance of the nesi'us (Rosh Hashanah 5654) is described as essentially hisbodedus: though he spoke Chassidus in public, and from 5650 also received people for yechidus, "nevertheless he was closed off for himself... the generality of the ten years was a way of hisbodedus (seclusion) — doing with himself and within himself" — before the turn to hisgalus [31641/hosafa_3 :: §61].

Solitude and intellect. The corpus gives hisbodedus a philosophical grounding: "intellect is for himself" — middos act and live only toward the other, but intellect needs no other. Rashbi in the cave, with no students, grew from twelve to twenty-four answers; "company often confuses." Hence "the early great ones' hisbodedus in fields and deserts": deep haskalah needs solitude and quieted faculties — "I was stunned for about an hour," the vessel raised to the intellect's source. Even the Sanhedrin deliberated each man alone [58841/maamar_009 :: §630]. Likewise: "Chochmah is only for himself, and the main is hisbodedus (solitude)" — exemplified by the Arizal, the Baal Shem Tov, and the Alter Rebbe; and Yackov — "because there was no waste" — "vayishkon betach badad," he dwelt securely alone (Tikkunei Zohar, tikkun 47) [58843/maamar_024 :: §2338].

Key distinctions

Hisbodedus vs. hisgalus. Seclusion is the mode of the hidden years — gathering, clarifying, doing "with himself and within himself"; revelation (hisgalus) is the mode that follows, as in the Rashab's turn of 5654 from hisbodedus to hisgalus [31641/hosafa_3 :: §61, §551].

Intellect vs. middos. The need for solitude belongs to intellect ("occupying in intelligibles is for himself specifically"), not to the middos, which exist only toward a zulas; without the other they can be nullified [31673/maamar_011 :: §1078; 58841/maamar_009 :: §961].

Two levels of need. The Shevatim needed deserts because their bitul was partial (the yesh's bitul); the Avos, whose bitul was bitul b'metzius, did not [31628/maamar_016 :: §4669]. Solitude is thus calibrated to the soul's level — a vessel for the avodah, not an end in itself.

Related concepts

  • Hisbonenus — contemplation, which hisbodedus shelters; deep haskalah requires solitude.
  • Bitul — the nullification the seclusion protects; yesh-bitul vs. metzius-bitul.
  • Nesi'us — the Rashab's hidden decade as preparation for leadership.
  • Midbar (desert) — the place of seclusion, free from disturbance.
