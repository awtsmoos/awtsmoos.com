Iskafya (אתכפיא) — Restraint / Subduing

Core definition

Iskafya — literally "coercion," "subduing" — is the avodah of restraining the nefesh habehamis: subduing the senses and middos so they do not act on their own impulses, without yet changing their inner nature. It is the first, indispensable stage of the soul's work: "first there must be the level of iskafya in all the senses and middot" [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 35], for drawing down the dwelling — "and I shall dwell within them" — is "through the service of iskafya (subduing) and ish'apcha (transforming)" [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 33].

How the Rebbeim develop it

The Zohar's promise. The Zohar teaches: כד אתכפיא סט״א אסתלק יקרא דקוב״ה בכולהו עלמין — "when the sitra achra is subdued, the Holy One's glory is elevated in all the worlds" [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 34]. Subduing evil, even without transforming it, elevates the Holy One's glory above.

The Mishkan's bars. The maamar "והבריח התיכון" (תרנ״ד) reads the Mishkan's bars as the two levels: the middle bar was inside the boards' thickness because it represents ish'apcha — inward transformation — while the outer bars represent iskafya — external subduing; "both are needed to make the middot a mishkan" [31641/maamar_022 :: Intro]. The boards are the middos, and the bars hold them upright — a dwelling for Divinity in the lower realms [31641/maamar_022 :: Summary (עמוד קסו)].

The parable of sitting back. Through contemplation of the Ohr Ein Sof's greatness, "the middot are called a chair and seat for the intellect — for the intellect shines and is enclothed in them, and they are drawn after the intellect, to love Hashem — except that at first it is in the state of iskafya — and like the parable of sitting on one's back (leaning back), which is the level of iskafya — and afterward one comes to ish'apcha" [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 350–356]. Sitting back: held in place by force, not yet walking forward on one's own — the precise image of restraint without transformation.

Iskafya in the order of prayer. Pesukei d'zimra is the nullification of foreign desires; then Kriyat Shema's love and fear; then Shemoneh Esrei's standing. "The love and fear of Kriyat Shema are still in the category of iskafya — since it is still in the existence of yesh — except that the yesh is nullified. And the standing of Shemoneh Esrei is the level of ish'apcha — nullification of existence" [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 771–776]. So "all this — pesukei d'zimra and Kriyat Shema — is in the category of iskafya, the nullification of the yesh; and afterward Shemoneh Esrei, true nullification" [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 777–781]. The outer bars are chochmah and binah (father and mother) working on the externality — Kriyat Shema's nullification of the yesh — while the middle bar is da'as within the boards: true nullification of existence [31641/maamar_022 :: Summary (עמוד קסו)].

Key distinctions

  • Iskafya vs. ish'apcha: iskafya = the yesh's nullification (the yesh remains, but is held back — "sitting on one's back"); ish'apcha = nullification of existence, the middos themselves transformed [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 354–356, 771–781].
  • Outer bars vs. middle bar: iskafya works on the externality (like bars outside the boards); ish'apcha works within the thickness of the boards — in the inwardness of the middos [31641/maamar_022 :: Intro, Summary (עמוד קסו)].
  • Order: iskafya always comes first — "first there must be the level of iskafya in all the senses and middot" [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 35]; only afterward does one come to ish'apcha [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 354–356].
  • Kriyat Shema vs. Shemoneh Esrei: Kriyat Shema's felt love and fear are still iskafya; Shemoneh Esrei's silent standing is ish'apcha [31641/maamar_022 :: עב׳ 771–781].

Related concepts

  • Ish'apcha (אתהפכא) — transformation; darkness turned to light.
  • Netzach — whose branch includes victory through coercion ("with the left repels — to coerce the opposing sitra achra") [15911/maamar_009 :: עב׳ 106–108].
  • Teshuvah — lower teshuvah in iskafya; higher teshuvah in ish'apcha.
  • Dirah baTachtonim — the dwelling in the lower realms, drawn through both avodos.
