Onesh (עונש) — punishment

Core definition

Onesh is Divine punishment — the suffering that follows sin, whether in this world or after death. The maamarim treat it not as retribution but as rectification: "if there must necessarily be some punishment, it is for the rectification of his soul — and this is not vengeance, Elokim forbid" [15893/maamar_001 :: Translation, עב׳ 263]. The proof is the Sages' teaching that when man suffers, the Shechinah suffers with him — "as it were: 'I am diminished from My head'" — "the Shechinah suffers too, as it is written, 'in all their affliction He was afflicted'" (Yeshaya 63:9) [15893/maamar_001 :: Translation, עב׳ 263]. Punishment is the Divine physician's treatment, not the king's revenge.

How the Rebbeim develop it

Cosmologically, reward and punishment operate at a specific level: "שכר and עונש drawn from da'as tachton's aspect, spreading and limited in middos and kelim" — as opposed to da'as elyon, which "is not drawn and spreading to enclothe in Atzilus's kelim and middos" [58845/maamar_018 :: [עמוד רא], עב׳ 57–60]. Reward and punishment belong to the world of measure — the lower knowledge, limited in vessels and attributes — where actions have proportionate consequences; above that level, "אני ה׳ לא שניתי" (Malachi 3:6) admits no change [58845/maamar_018 :: [עמוד רא], עב׳ 59]. Punishment is thus a measured response within the system of vessels, not an event in the Essence.

In avodah, yiras ha'onesh (fear of punishment) is the lower of the two fears: "there are two kinds of yirah: yiras ha'onesh and yiras haromemus (fear of exaltedness)" — and "yirah is easier for a man to draw upon himself than love," since "in yirah there is also yiras ha'onesh" [58843/maamar_032 :: עב׳ 98–100]. It is the accessible beginning of fear, to be transcended toward the fear of His exaltedness.

The postmortem order is described in a Yiddish passage: "after a man's long days there are two punishments before the punishment of Gehenom: one punishment is chibbut ha'kever (the grave's throes), and the second is kaf ha'kela (the sling): where they throw the soul from one end of the world to the other" [31682/maamar_021 :: עב׳ 162–163]. The parable: "a garment full of stains and dust must be washed — either with cold or with hot water — to remove the stains; but first one must shake it out and beat out the dust; and then wash it" [31682/maamar_021 :: עב׳ 164–165]. The punishments are the shaking and the washing — the soul's cleansing, not its destruction.

Key distinctions

  • Rectification vs. vengeance: "if there must necessarily be some punishment, it is for the rectification of his soul — and this is not vengeance, Elokim forbid" — the Shechinah suffering alongside proves it [15893/maamar_001 :: Translation, עב׳ 263].
  • Da'as tachton vs. da'as elyon: reward and punishment flow from the lower knowledge, "spreading and limited in middos and kelim" — the realm of measure; the higher knowledge is unenclothed, admitting no change [58845/maamar_018 :: [עמוד רא], עב׳ 57–60].
  • Yiras ha'onesh vs. yiras haromemus: fear of punishment — the lower, easier fear — vs. fear of His exaltedness [58843/maamar_032 :: עב׳ 99–100].
  • The three postmortem stages: chibbut ha'kever, then kaf ha'kela, and only then Gehenom — shaking out the dust before washing the garment [31682/maamar_021 :: עב׳ 162–165].

Related concepts

  • Yirah (fear): the lower fear as the accessible beginning of the heart's service [58843/maamar_032 :: עב׳ 98–100].
  • Teshuvah: the rectification punishment serves — and which can avert it.
  • Gehinnom: the final cleansing, after the two preliminary punishments [31682/maamar_021 :: עב׳ 162].
  • Shechinah's suffering: "in all their affliction He was afflicted" — the proof that punishment is not vengeance [15893/maamar_001 :: Translation, עב׳ 263].
