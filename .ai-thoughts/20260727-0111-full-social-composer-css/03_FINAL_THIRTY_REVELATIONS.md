2. Alias identity should be visible but quiet.

4. Publication is a gate, not another form section.
5. Preview should read like the post, not like JSON inspection.
6. Technical payloads remain available but visually subordinate.
7. Semantic accent rails are more useful than colored panel backgrounds.
8. Violet belongs to writing and structure.
9. Cyan belongs to identity and destination.
10. Amber belongs to publication and delivery.
11. Green belongs to completed readiness.
12. Red belongs to errors and destructive actions only.
13. One control system must cover inputs, selects, textareas, and buttons.
14. Checkboxes need alignment and readable labels.
15. Details summaries need open-state evidence.
16. Structured sections need chapter numbers and clear inner zones.
17. Per-section media must feel attached to that section.
18. Playlist selection should communicate hierarchy and final destination.
19. Playlist creation must not visually compete with choosing an existing list.
20. Mobile writing must remain first in DOM presentation order.
21. Mobile identity belongs in one compact row.
22. Mobile publication belongs above the safe area.
23. Mobile preview is a temporary full-screen reading layer.
24. Desktop preview should remain sticky but not visually dominant over writing.
25. Long labels must truncate without losing controls.
26. All hover effects must be pointer-qualified.
27. Accessibility states must be explicit and tested.
28. Every owner must remain below 120 lines.
29. Browser evidence must compare layout viewport to layout viewport.
30. No selector used by JavaScript may be renamed or removed.
